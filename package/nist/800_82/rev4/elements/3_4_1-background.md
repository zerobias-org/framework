The OT cybersecurity program should be continually observed to determine its overall effectiveness. This may include evaluating the current charter and related policies to determine whether they remain the most appropriate components.

The organization should define both key performance indicators (KPIs) and key risk indicators (KRIs), analyzing them over time to gauge the program’s performance and the organization’s risk posture. While KPIs are quantitative measures of the program’s operational efficiency, KRIs may result from shortcomings in the governance program.

Examples of KPIs include:

- Percentage of OT assets with applied system hardening
- Mean time to detect incidents (MTTD)
- Mean time to respond to incidents (MTTR)
- Percentage of security policies reviewed and reapproved in the past 12 months
- Personnel security awareness training completion rate
- Percentage of personnel who reported phishing simulations

Examples of KRIs include:

- Percentage of production assets deemed end-of-service (EOS) or end-of-life (EOL) by vendors
- Percentage of personnel who have not completed security awareness training
- Percentage of personnel who failed phishing simulations
- Number of open, critical vulnerabilities yet to be remediated

The organization should define KPI targets and KRI thresholds to better manage the program throughout its life cycle. While a KPI target is a numerical value that the organization hopes to meet or exceed (e.g., 90 % of OT assets are hardened), a KRI threshold is an expression of the maximum numerical value before risk is deemed unacceptable (e.g., no more than 5 % of staff should have missed or failed their security awareness training).
