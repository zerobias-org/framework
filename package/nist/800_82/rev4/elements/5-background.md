When designing a security architecture for an OT environment, it is generally recommended to separate the OT network from the enterprise network. The nature of network traffic on these two network types differs significantly. Internet access, email, and remote access are typically permitted on the enterprise network but not allowed on OT networks. There may also be differences in the degree of rigor associated with enterprise and OT environment change control procedures. Additionally, using the enterprise network for OT communication protocols could expose OT components to cyber attacks, such as the denial of service, adversary-in-the-middle, or other network-based attacks. Utilizing separate networks allows for greater flexibility to address the security and performance requirements of each environment independently. This separation should extend beyond the boundary between enterprise and OT networks. Within the OT environment itself, networks that serve different functions and purposes warrant their own segmentation. Operational networks (i.e., those that carry process control traffic between supervisory systems and field devices) have fundamentally different traffic characteristics, availability requirements, and security properties than networks dedicated to management functions, such as configuration, patching, and access control. Combining these on a shared network infrastructure may introduce risk to operational traffic from management activity, limit the ability to apply independent security policies to each function, and create conditions in which a security event that affects one network type can propagate to the other. Additionally, external parties (e.g., vendors, other third parties) should have access paths that are architecturally separate from both operational and internal management traffic with their own credential stores, entry points, and enforcement boundaries so that the trust level extended to external access does not inadvertently reach internal functions through shared infrastructure.

Practical considerations (e.g., digital transformation, the cost of OT installation, maintaining a homogenous network infrastructure) often mean that connections are required between OT networks and other IT networks. These connections represent additional risk, and organizations should seek to minimize them and implement additional security controls where they exist.
