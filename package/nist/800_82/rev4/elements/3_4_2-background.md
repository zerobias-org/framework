Organizations must establish oversight capabilities and teams to monitor risk management activities and make necessary decisions, investments, and strategies. These teams should help review key artifacts and indicators from the risk management process, including incidents, vulnerabilities, audit findings, and assessments to track the status of the security program. An organization-wide risk management strategy is foundational to developing an OT cybersecurity strategy. 5 The OT cybersecurity strategy leverages the organization-wide risk management strategy — including organization-defined risk tolerance, threats, assumptions, constraints, priorities, and trade-offs — to further tailor the strategy to apply to the OT cybersecurity program. By consciously choosing to develop and implement a cybersecurity strategy, an organization establishes a disciplined approach that considers all aspects of the system life cycle, from procurement to decommissioning, with cybersecurity in mind. As a result, the organization can ensure that cybersecurity goals are realized in its systems. The adoption of a cybersecurity strategy can result in a more systematic implementation of risk decisions into system development and operation. Decisions on cybersecurity strategy should flow from a high-level understanding of the organization’s operations, objectives, and cybersecurity goals. A comprehensive and accepted cybersecurity strategy can assist an organization with consistently maintaining acceptable risk management throughout the life cycle of an OT system.

The OT cybersecurity strategy should:

- Refine the OT operation’s organizational risk tolerance, which drives the OT cybersecurity operation’s priorities
- Refine and supplement guidance from the organization-wide risk management strategy to address OT-specific constraints and requirements
- Identify the OT cybersecurity team and personnel and the associated OT-specific cybersecurity training and awareness necessary
- Address the OT cybersecurity operation model (e.g., insource, outsource, use managed security services)
- Outline the appropriate cybersecurity architecture for the various OT sites within the OT program.
- Address both IT and OT concerns and requirements; while IT may prioritize data loss or system availability, OT may prioritize system safety, production efficiency, and environmental protection
- Incorporate critical infrastructure standards and regulatory requirements, which are typically designed to protect critical cyber assets to support reliability and may carry additional legal obligations for the organization
- State the need for sound cybersecurity practices, such as patching or monitoring

The strategy should provide guidance on how the organization will make key cybersecurity decisions, including:

- Architectural decisions informed by a cybersecurity to increase the likelihood that high-level cybersecurity goals will be reflected in the cybersecurity of individual systems, including the approach to securing integration with other networks and systems (e.g., business networks, cloud, IIoT technologies)
- The management of OT systems across the life cycle, including the approach to addressing products that pass end-of-life status or have identified cybersecurity or supply chain concerns
- The required approval, evaluation, or restrictions necessary to inform the adoption and use of new technologies (e.g., crypto agility, artificial intelligence [AI], and machine learning [ML] technologies, digital twins)
- Data governance issues, especially those associated with data hosted on IT environments, and any third parties (e.g., contractors, integrators)
