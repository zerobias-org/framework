A comprehensive understanding and baseline of the data flows associated with the assets is critical for the successful deployment of cybersecurity controls. This information is also important for other security functions, including network monitoring and incident response. To establish a baseline, organizations should begin by understanding which devices a specific asset communicates with; which addresses, ports, and protocols it uses; and the functional purpose of the communication. This information should be documented within a broad network diagram to ensure that it can be broadly shared and communicated with individuals across the organization. While establishing baselines for all devices across an organization may not be feasible, organizations should prioritize focusing on critical assets, network segments, and areas in which communications cross key boundaries, such as connectivity with the DMZ, internet, and external parties.

Numerous tools can support the development of an architecture document, including tools to perform asset discovery and management (Sec. 4.1.1) and network detection tools (Sec. 4.3.1.4). These tools can help an organization identify, document, and diagram the interconnections between devices, enterprise networks, and other external connections. Organizations should pay particular attention to cloud conduits when documenting their architecture. Cloud conduits are connections between OT systems and cloud-based services or platforms that may bypass traditional network boundaries and introduce exposure that is not immediately visible in standard network diagrams. These connections should be explicitly identified, documented, and assessed as part of the baseline process.

Organizations should also treat the discovery of unknown assets and undocumented connections as a core objective of the architecture and baseline process. Unauthorized or undocumented devices and connections, sometimes referred to as Shadow IT/OT, can introduce unmanaged risks into the OT environment. Any device or connection that cannot be attributed to a known, authorized function should be investigated and either formally documented or removed.
