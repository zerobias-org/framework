Organizations should manage cybersecurity supply chain risks arising from products, services, suppliers, and third parties that support OT environments. Because OT systems often depend on specialized equipment, limited supplier options, vendor-maintained services, and remote access by external parties, supply chain risks can directly affect the availability, integrity, and confidentiality of OT systems and data with possible consequences ranging from operational disruption to safety impacts. To address these risks, organizations should incorporate C-SCRM into their broader governance, procurement, and life cycle management practices, including identifying critical suppliers, defining required supplier security practices, and managing supplier and product risk throughout the system life cycle.

Supply chain risks can include the insertion of counterfeits, unauthorized production, malicious insiders, tampering, theft, the insertion of malicious software and hardware, and poor manufacturing and development practices across the products or services acquired to support OT needs. Whether they are malicious, natural, or unintentional, these risks have the potential to compromise the availability and integrity of critical OT systems and components as well as the availability, integrity, and confidentiality of the data utilized by the OT, thereby causing harms that range from minor disruptions to impacts on life and safety. OT organizations have key challenges with managing supply chain risks, including:

- Organizations often have sector-specific equipment requirements and depend on a smaller number of key suppliers. Therefore, organizations may be required to decide whether to accept or manage the risk posed by suppliers that do not meet organizational requirements.
- Many suppliers may require remote access to the OT environment, including MSPs that need remote access to OT networks or vendors that require access to data from their equipment within the OT network.
- OT environments must often integrate new equipment or systems that consist of many diverse digital devices (e.g., cranes, electric transformers, water treatment systems). The organization must track and validate the supply chain risk of that equipment and all associated digital components.

Identifying, assessing, and effectively responding to cybersecurity risks in supply chains is best accomplished by incorporating C-SCRM considerations into organizational policies, plans, and practices.
