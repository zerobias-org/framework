Organizations must manage supply chain risks across the product life cycle. This should include documented processes to validate the authenticity and integrity of products, control supplier access, and assess supplier and product risks. Using these processes, organizations should vet suppliers and service providers to ascertain their capabilities, trustworthiness, the adequacy of their internal security practices, the effectiveness of their safeguards, their supply chain relationships, and any risks that may be associated with those relationships and dependencies. Examples of key checks that the organization should validate include:

- Whether the products include any unauthorized components, software, suppliers, or regions for all acquisitions
- Remote access and on-site management requirements and mechanisms to audit and control access
- The existence of unauthorized communication devices (e.g., cellular modems)

Additionally, special consideration should be given to how difficult it may be to obtain original replacement parts or updates over the life of the product and how diverse the sources of supply are and may be in the future.

Supplemental guidance for supply chain risk management can be found in the following documents:

- SP 800-161r1, Cybersecurity Supply Chain Risk Management Practices for Systems and Organizations
- IR 8276, Key Practices in Cyber Supply Chain Risk Management: Observations from Industry
- CISA Software Acquisition Guide Fact Sheet
- CISA Software Bill of Materials (SBOM)
- CISA A Hardware Bill of Materials Framework for Supply Chain Risk Management Fact Sheet
