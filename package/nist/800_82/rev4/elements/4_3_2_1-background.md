Organizations should define how security events are integrated into their security operations center (SOC) or network operations center (NOC) functions. In some organizations, OT monitoring is handled by a dedicated OT security team, while in others it is integrated into an existing IT SOC or NOC. Regardless of the model, the roles and responsibilities for monitoring, triaging, and escalating OT network alerts should be clearly defined and documented. Organizations should develop a plan to identify their available log sources and establish a centralized collection and aggregation capability that consolidates them into a unified platform for analysis, correlation, and retention. A SIEM platform can accomplish this by providing organizations with a single location to aggregate logs, apply correlation rules, and maintain a searchable record of security-relevant activities across the OT environment.

A significant challenge in SOC/NOC integration is the context gap between security analysts and OT operations personnel. Security analysts may have limited familiarity with OT protocols, equipment, and operational processes, which can make it difficult to accurately assess the significance of an alert without an operations context. Conversely, OT operations personnel may not have the security expertise to recognize threat patterns or assess the implications of anomalous network activity. Effective SOC/NOC integration requires close collaboration between security and operations teams, and organizations should establish clear escalation paths that involve operations personnel in the triage and assessment of OT network alerts before containment or response actions are taken.

Physical access, network, and host logs should be forwarded to the SIEM to enable this type of time-based correlation. Defining alert logic around physical-cyber event sequences adds a detection layer that is difficult for an adversary to avoid without simultaneously defeating physical access controls and operating within expected cyber behavioral patterns. Organizations should work with both physical security and OT operations teams to define what normal physical-cyber activity sequences look like so that anomalies can be identified with appropriate context.
