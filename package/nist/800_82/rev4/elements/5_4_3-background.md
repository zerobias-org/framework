If a process can be safely operated using non-digital instrumentation and controls, maintaining an analog backup (e.g., hardwired instrumentation, physical gauges, manual control stations that do not depend on the digital control system) provides a recovery path that is unaffected by a cyber incident against the digital environment. Analog backups are most relevant for safety-critical or highly critical processes for which the consequences of extended digital system unavailability are unacceptable. They should be evaluated as a complement rather than a replacement for digital backup and restoration processes.

Digital backup and restoration practices (e.g., layered, time-sequenced “backup-in-depth” approach with local, facility, and disaster-recovery backups) are addressed at a general level in SP 800-82r3, Sec. 5.3.2.1. Broader contingency planning guidelines, including backup strategy selection and testing, is provided in SP 800-34r1, Contingency Planning Guide for Federal Information Systems.
