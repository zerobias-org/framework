The CSF 2.0 Identity Management, Authentication, and Access Control (PR.AA) controls ensure that access to physical and logical assets is strictly limited to authorized users, services, and hardware and is managed commensurate with the assessed risk of unauthorized access. Establishing a robust access control architecture in an operational environment is a multi-step process that encompasses the full life cycle of an identity. It begins with foundational identity and access management (IAM) to administer accounts and assign permissions, followed by the deployment of appropriate authentication mechanisms to reliably verify those identities. Once verified, authorization and logical access controls ensure that local and remote users are granted only the minimum privileges necessary to perform their duties. Furthermore, because gaining physical access to an OT control room or component often implies gaining logical access to the system, these digital safeguards must be seamlessly integrated with comprehensive physical access controls.

While enterprise IT environments typically rely on stringent, ubiquitous access controls, implementing these capabilities in OT environments requires careful tailoring to avoid adversely impacting continuous physical processes. Organizations must balance the need to secure systems against the imperative for immediate operator access during emergency situations. In critical situations, the time required to enter a user’s credentials may impede rapid response or intervention, resulting in negative consequences for safety, health, or the environment. When legacy OT equipment cannot natively support modern IAM capabilities, or when strict access controls introduce unacceptable latency or safety risks, organizations should select compensating countermeasures to provide an equivalent level of protection for the OT environment.
