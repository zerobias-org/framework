This section presents the functions and architectural patterns that support a defensible OT cybersecurity architecture, organized into three categories.

OT/ICS system management (Table 7) and security controls (Table 8) are functions: discrete operational capabilities that produce a specific security-relevant outcome. System management functions establish and maintain visibility into managed assets and govern the introduction of change. Security controls enforce protection at points where access, transfer, or observation occurs. Architecture patterns (Table 9) are distinct from these functions. Rather than performing an operation, an architecture pattern defines the structural context in which functions are deployed and enforced, determining where a given function applies and what it is permitted to reach.

Each function and pattern addresses one or more outcomes defined in the OT Cybersecurity Outcomes and Objectives framework referenced throughout this document and was selected for its role in mitigating risk characteristics of OT environments, including insufficient asset visibility, uncontrolled configuration drift, ungoverned content ingress, propagation of credential compromise across trust domains, and the absence of independent, tamper-resistant monitoring.

No individual function or pattern is sufficient in isolation. System management and security controls are interdependent: system management provides the visibility and governance records that security controls act on. Architecture patterns establish the structural conditions that require clearly defined boundaries, controlled and auditable access paths, and assurance that compromise of one component does not silently defeat protections implemented elsewhere.

Table 7 through Table 9 identify each function and architecture pattern, together with a description and reference to related guidance presented elsewhere in this document. Section 5.2 presents representative example architectures demonstrating how these may be composed, sequenced, and enforced in combination.

Not every item identified in Table 7 through Table 9 will be applicable to every environment. Asset owners and operators should evaluate applicability against the criticality of the assets being protected, the operational constraints of the environment, and organizational risk tolerance, consistent with the risk-informed approach described throughout this document.
