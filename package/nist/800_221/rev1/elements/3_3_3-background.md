The next key input to risk identification is understanding the potential conditions that enable a threat event to occur. It is important to consider all types of vulnerabilities in all assets, including people, facilities, and information. For this document, vulnerability is simply a condition that enables a threat event to occur. It could be an unpatched software flaw, a raw material limitation, a process that leads to human error, or a physical environmental condition (e.g., a wooden structure being flammable). Moreover, a threat that does not have a corresponding vulnerability may not result in a negative risk. Identifying negative risks includes understanding the potential threats and vulnerabilities to organizational assets, which can then be used to develop scenarios that describe potential risks.

While automated scanners and techniques may help to quickly identify some common vulnerabilities (e.g., software flaws, misconfigurations), ICT weaknesses are not limited to enterprise hardware and software. Advanced exploitable conditions can be determined by using system engineering analysis tools, such as Failure Mode, Effects & Criticality Analysis (FMECA), described in ISO/International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) 31010 [IEC31010]. For the ICT risk disciplines of privacy, supply chain, and cybersecurity, the controls described in [SP80053] can help highlight many potential weaknesses. Whether caused by system failures or adversarial attack, such weaknesses — including critical timing race conditions and interactive system instabilities — can trigger emergent or systemic risks and should be considered part of a comprehensive threat analysis.
