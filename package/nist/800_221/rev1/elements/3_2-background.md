The COSO framework Principle 3 emphasizes the need for enterprise leaders to “define the desired behaviors that characterize the entity’s desired culture” regarding risk, including ICTRM [COSOERM]. Culture represents the collective values, attitudes, and behaviors that characterize how an organization considers risk in its day-to-day activities and strategic decisions. Culture shapes how people identify and escalate risks, whether risk information flows freely to decision-makers, and whether governance structures function as intended. ISACA’s Digital Trust Ecosystem Framework (DTEF) includes culture as an important factor in establishing and maintaining digital trust [DTEF]. It highlights the need to identify and evaluate the organization’s existing risk culture, modify governance to establish and communicate the desired risk culture, promote that culture through active training and communication practices, and promote cultural feedback consistently.

As with other governance elements, senior leadership’s commitment to an effective organizational risk culture directly influences how risk awareness and accountability are practiced throughout the enterprise. Therefore, enterprises should assess and actively shape their risk culture as part of ERM program development.
