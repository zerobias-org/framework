ICTRM and ERM have several points of integration. First, enterprise governance activities for ERM direct the strategies and methods for ICTRM and other risk management disciplines to use. Based on these guidelines, each discipline within each organization uses risk registers to document its risks. In the case of ICTRM, risks are derived from system-level assessments. These risk registers are then aggregated, normalized, and used to create enterprise-level risk registers for each discipline. These, in turn, become part of a broader enterprise risk register (ERR) that encompasses all disciplines.

As shown in Fig. 3, ERM and ICTRM are not separate processes. Rather, ICTRM represents an important subset of the broader portfolio of ERM. Documenting and tracking ICT risks in lower-level risk registers supports better management of ICT risks at the enterprise level.

The ERR is prioritized by those with fiduciary and oversight responsibilities to create an enterprise risk profile (ERP), also known as an ERM risk profile. An ERP is created by considering enterprise risks in relation to achieving objectives as typically outlined in an organizational strategic plan. ERPs commonly include four kinds of objectives: strategic, operations (operational effectiveness and efficiency), reporting (reporting reliability), and compliance (compliance with applicable laws and regulations). While there may be some overlap among the categories of objectives, understanding uncertainty as it affects these objectives will help inform timely decision-making. Effective ERM balances achieving objectives with optimizing resources.
